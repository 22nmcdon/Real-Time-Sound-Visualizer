The EQ’s five bands, each its own

A strip of the five bands across the top of the editor, each in its own ink, with its switch. Each band’s own curve is drawn in that ink, and the chosen one is washed. Each point sits on its own values, so it stays under the hand to the edge.

Live. The window is Space & Memory as built, in the boxes scope.html measures at 60c9db7 (checks/real_boxes.py). The editor opens over the tabs where it opens today, at the same size. The curves are the page’s own arithmetic, Web Audio’s biquads as pieces/eq.py holds them, at 44.1 kHz. Nothing in scope.html or the core has changed. Drag a point, press a cell, turn a knob, double-click a point to reset it. With a point chosen, the arrows move it.

What changes

  • The band strip. One cell for each band, in the range’s order: Low cut, Low shelf, Mid, High shelf, High cut. Each cell has a rule of its ink along the top, its shape drawn small in its ink, its name, its lamp (the switch) and its values in one line. A shelf or the mid shows 160 Hz · +4.5 dB; a cut shows 12 kHz · Q 0.71. Pressing a cell chooses the band. Pressing its lamp switches the band on or off and leaves which one is chosen alone. Today’s editor has no per-band switch: a band was out at 0 dB, and a cut was out at its end.
  • Each band its own ink, the same for its cell, its point, its curve and its knobs’ rings. See the colours below.
  • The plot. Each band’s own curve is drawn thin in its ink. The sum is on top in charcoal, as today. The chosen band’s curve is drawn stronger, and the area between it and 0 dB is washed lightly in its ink. A band switched off draws no curve, and its point is a hollow dashed ring in grey, still there to take hold of. The EQ’s own Off greys the sum, as today.
  • The points sit on their own values, not on the sum. Across is the band’s frequency. Up and down is where the band’s own curve crosses that frequency. For the mid that is its gain. For a shelf it is half its gain, where the shelf is half-way up its step. For a cut it is its resonance, 20 log₁₀ Q, from −6 dB at Q 0.5 to +12 dB at Q 4, the scale the cuts already use. So every point sits exactly on its own coloured line. The pointer is mapped straight to the values, so the point stays under the hand. Past the plot’s edge it stops at the edge, where it can still be taken hold of. Switch Points sit to today and drag the High cut to the right to see the old way: the point leaves the pointer and rides the high shelf’s step. A shelf’s point moves half as far as its gain does, which keeps it on its own line, so it stops at +6 and −8 on the plot when its gain reaches the knob’s ends, +12 and −16. A cut’s point stops at +12 and −6, at Q 4 and 0.5.
  • Only the chosen band’s knobs are in the foot, under its name and in its ink. That is two knobs, or three for the mid, in place of eleven in a row. The other knobs go back to the EQ’s hidden store while the editor is open, as they do when it is shut, so every id still resolves for the plugin’s panel, the morph and the host. The foot now has room for a line on what the points do. That line went with the cuts on 23 Nov, when the knobs took the row.

The colours

The five lane inks, slate to terracotta: no new token. DESIGN.md section 10 calls them “a register the whole Bench draws from, not only the traces’ colours”. Their all-pairs separation was measured as a six, and these are five of the six, so the measurement already covers them. Charcoal, the sixth, stays the sum’s. They are ordered so that bands next to each other in the range, which overlap the most, are never the two greens or the two warm inks.

One clash to know about. With two hands and the EQ unlinked, today’s editor dashes the other hand’s curve in that hand’s ink. The upper hand’s ink is olive, the low cut’s. The dash tells them apart, and a band’s curve is thin and a hand’s is not. If that is not enough, the other hand’s curve can go grey and dashed while the bands show.

What is stored new

Five switches, one per band, and their upper-hand twins: eqCutLowOn, eqLowOn, eqMidOn, eqHighOn, eqCutHighOn, and bEqCutLowOn to bEqCutHighOn, under the EQ’s link as its other fields are. They are additive and on by default, so an old setup has all five on and plays to the bit as before. There is no version bump and no decode pass, since Object.assign over the defaults gives an old code the new default. A code writes a band’s switch only when it is off.

In the chain, a band switched off is skipped, the same as a band at 0 dB or a cut at its end today. The EQ’s own eqOn stays the switch for the whole EQ, a field, as the owner chose on 17 Nov. Moving a point of a band that is off switches that band on, as moving a point while the EQ is off switches the EQ on today.

What I would change my mind on

  • A cut at its end, switched on. It still does nothing, as today: a low cut at 20 Hz is out of the chain. Its lamp says on and its point sits at the edge. I could make the cuts start off at a sensible place (a low cut at 30 Hz, a high cut at 18 kHz), so that switching one on does something at once. That would mean a default that differs from an old code’s. It would be a version bump with a pass that writes on where a cut was in, or off where it was at its end. I lean towards it, but it is your call.
  • The knobs’ rings in the band’s ink rather than the accent. This ties a knob to its point. It is the one place a band’s ink stands where the house has blush-deep, and that is local to this editor.
  • The EQ’s line in the rack could carry five small dots in the inks in place of today’s three black ones. I have left that alone here.
