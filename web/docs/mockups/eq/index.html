The EQ, drawn

Its response as the face of it: a small curve on the rack's line where the three gain knobs were, and a large one to drag, opened over the tabs with the screen still in view.

Everything here is live. The curve is worked out with the core's own formulas (the Audio EQ Cookbook, shelves at slope one, the mid's Q from its width by the analog relation, at 48 kHz), so what you drag is what the instrument would play. The screen's figure is a fifth, A3 against E4, through this EQ. Nothing in scope.html or the core has changed.

What to try

  • Drag a point. Sideways is where it acts, up and down its gain. The low shelf runs 40–640 Hz, the mid 125 Hz–8 kHz, the high shelf 1–16 kHz, each in the knob's own hundred steps, so a point can only land where the knob can.
  • The mid's width: Shift-drag it up and down, or the wheel over it, from a fifth of an octave to four. Its width is drawn as a bracket under it while it is held or chosen.
  • Double-click a point to put it back where it starts. Right-click one (or a long press, or Shift+F10) for Type a value, Reset and What is this.
  • Keyboard: Tab to a point, arrows move it (left and right five of the knob's steps, up and down half a decibel), Shift finer (one step, a tenth of a decibel), [ and ] the mid's width.
  • Turn a knob under the curve: the point follows, because the knobs are the values and the points only write them.
  • Close the editor and press the little curve on the EQ's line to open it again. Switch the EQ off and its line goes flat and grey; dragging any point off nought switches it back on, as moving a gain does today.

The choices made

On the line
The curve, 150 by 34, in place of the three gain knobs, with the three points as dots. It is a button: pressing it opens the editor. It stays on the folded line too (96 by 22), because it is the line's reading rather than a control, and a folded EQ that showed nothing would say nothing. The words beside it (low +3.0 dB at 160 Hz · …) stay; they are what a screen reader and a narrow window keep.
The editor
Over the tabs, where the modulation editor opens, so the screen stays in view while you listen and watch. A log-frequency grid from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, ±12 dB, the sum of the bands in charcoal and each band's own in faint grey under it. The chosen point is in the accent, the others charcoal on paper; each sits at its corner frequency and its full gain, so a shelf's curve passes half-way through it, as the info line says.
The knobs stay
All seven, under the curve, as the values: a setup, a host knob, undo, the morph and modulation read and write them exactly as now, and the points write the knobs. The knobs a point moves are washed while it is chosen. Modulation stays on the knobs (their right-click), not on the points, because a point is two or three controls at once.
The hands
With two hands the link sits in the editor's head, after the name: Same in both, or the edited hand's own in its ink. Unlinked, the other hand's curve is drawn faint and dashed in its ink behind, so the two can be compared without switching. The rack's line keeps its short pill (Both, Lower, Upper) before the arrow, as now.
The spectrum
Optional, faint, behind the curve, on a scale of its own (60 dB from the top of the plot to its foot). Here it is an illustration, not live: the fifth on the screen, eight harmonics a note falling 4.5 dB per harmonic, as the EQ leaves it. Live it would be the sound's own, as the Spect view reads it.

The one alternative

A · Over the tabs
Recommended. The largest curve the column allows - its plot 752 by 452 of the page's pixels at 1400 × 900, and 654 by 343 at 960 × 600 - and the same place and the same Close as the modulation editor, so it is one thing to learn. Its cost: while it is open the other modules and the tab cards are covered, so switching the delay on means closing it first.
B · In the rack
The EQ's arrow opens the curve in the module's rest, the others folding to their lines above and below it. The rack and its order stay in view and every switch is still a press away. Its cost: the curve gets what height is left after five folded lines - a plot 185 px tall at 1400 × 900 and 99 at 960 × 600 (under half A's height, and under a third), which is little room for ±12 dB over ten octaves - and the tab's fit becomes the EQ's problem: any line added to the rack comes out of the curve.

How the curve was checked

The page's response (the function eqResponseDb in this page) was compared with the core's: a small C++ program that includes core/include/scope/plane.h, takes each band's coefficients from PlaneEngine::eqBand (the core's own, public for its tests) and evaluates the cascade at the same frequencies. Six settings, 241 frequencies each from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, at 48 kHz and 44.1 kHz; the null is the page given the mid's width as Q unconverted, which must miss. The core's numbers were printed to nine decimals, so a difference of 5 × 10−10 dB is that printing's rounding: the two agree to the last digit printed, far inside the 0.01 dB asked. The null misses by 0.67 dB or more wherever the mid is in, so the check can fail. (A setting with the mid at nought has no width to get wrong, so its null is a dash.)

Not decided here

  • Whether the curve on the line should draw each hand's when the hands differ. Here it draws the edited hand's only.
  • Whether a point's drag is a step in undo on release (it would be, as a knob's drag is: the knobs are what undo records).
  • The live spectrum's cost: the Spect view already has an analyser, but on the editor it would run with the Bench open, at the editor's size.
