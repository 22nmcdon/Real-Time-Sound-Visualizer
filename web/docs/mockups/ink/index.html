What Ink is

  • A figure area and nothing else. Ink has no band and no settings row, so the area takes the whole tab. Its bar has two subtabs, each label its name and a summary: Oscillator · 3:2 · FM 2.0, Shaping · drive 45% · 4 bits, or plain and clean when nothing is turned. The summaries shorten as Play’s do, and none needed to at the six windows.
  • Oscillator draws both channels over the voice’s whole pattern: the left in charcoal and the right in slate (offset by Phase), with the plain shape dashed and grey behind them, so what the oscillator adds is the difference you see. The span is as many of the note’s cycles as it takes to repeat: the Ratio’s denominator, and the sub’s octave (2 cycles: the pattern repeats every 2). Unison is drawn drifting while the clock runs. Under the drawing is one row: Frequency, Phase, Ratio, FM, Warp, Warp at, Ring, Sync, Sub, Unison and Spread. Warp at and Spread are greyed, not hidden, while Warp and Unison are off. The sub’s octave and shape go to the section’s dots menu.
  • Shaping draws the shaper’s curve, a square of in against out, with the straight line dashed behind it. The Crush’s bits are the steps the curve takes, and the smooth curve stays faint under the steps. Beside it, two cycles of the oscillator before and after, with Rate shown as the holds it puts in at the note’s Frequency. Under them: Drive, Fold, Crush and Rate.

Drawn from the core

Every line comes from the core’s formulas, ported line for line and checked against the core itself. checks/core_ref.cpp is built against core/include and runs the real oscStep, shapeSample and setShaping. The checks compare each drawing with it, point for point:

  • FM, Ring, Warp on the sine, Unison two seconds in, and the harmonic at 7:2: within 1e-9.
  • Sync and the square: within 0.0013 away from their edges. The core band-limits the edges and plays sync a sample late.
  • Warp on the triangle: within 0.002. The core takes the bend’s offset out with a 5 Hz blocker, where the drawing takes out its settled mean.
  • The shaper’s curve: to the last bit.

The check found one thing about the core: its sub runs one sample ahead of the note. It steps before it reads, which puts it 0.0072 rad early at 48 kHz, two octaves down. That is inaudible, but it is a difference of 1.3e-4 against the drawing.

The wave or the figure: measured

Each control was turned from nought, and the movement of each drawing’s outline was measured in pixels on a 300 px drawing:

ControlWave, Phase 90°Figure, Phase 90°Figure, Phase 0°Wave, sawFigure, saw

I chose the wave, even though at the default Phase of 90° the figure moves as much or more. There are three reasons:

  • The screen beside it already draws the figure, live and large. A second copy in the figure area tells the player nothing the screen does not.
  • At Phase 0° the figure is blind. The two channels are then one signal, so the figure is the diagonal whatever FM, Warp, Ring, Sync, Sub or Unison do. The checks find it exactly on the diagonal for all seven, while the wave moves 14 to 44 px.
  • The wave keeps a before. The plain shape is drawn behind, so Sync’s restarts, Sub’s slow swell and Ring’s envelope read as what each adds. A figure has no before to compare against.

The wave and the figure above puts both in one row, and it costs the wave its width: 441 × 294 at 960 × 600 against 689 × 336.

Nothing moves: every box on Ink stays where it is, to half a pixel, across nine settings, five shapes and both subtabs. The check found one real shift: the key over the wave said the plain sine or the plain triangle and changed its width. It is one width now.

What I would change my mind on

  • Shaping on a tall window leaves paper. The curve is square and the cycle is held to 1.5:1, so at 1660 × 1050 there are 273 px under them (123 at 1400 × 900). Stacking the cycle over the curve when there is the height, as the filter’s two drawings stack, would use the paper. I kept one layout until the owner has seen it.
  • The figure as well, at Phase 90° on a wide window, if the owner wants Ink to stand on its own when the screen is in Y–T or Spect.
  • Frequency on Ink. It changes nothing in the wave’s shape, only its milliseconds. It belongs with the note’s settings on Play; I kept it here only because Ink has it today.
