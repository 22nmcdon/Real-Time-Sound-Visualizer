Why the small drawing felt off

Looking at the first mockup's block at both windows, five things, in order of how much they mattered:

  1. Straight time hid the attack. 8 ms of a 308 ms shape is under 3 px at 960 × 600, so every shape began with a wall, and a quick pluck and a soft swell started the same way.
  2. A 60 dB axis on 60 px flattened the decay and made the release a cliff. A sustain at −3 dB is 5 per cent of the height down, so the decay read as a plateau and the whole shape as a box; and the old release, aimed only 0.2 below silence, falls its last 30 dB in its last tenth of time, which a separate band for silence under a −60 floor drew as a wall at the end.
  3. The held gap was a dashed rule across the drawing, as wide as the attack, splitting it in two at the one place nothing happens.
  4. It was a chart in a box: a pink fill, gridlines and a frame, stretched to the column's width at about 3½ to 1, with its numbers in a table underneath, read separately.
  5. It was a button with nothing to say so but a word on hover.

All three styles below change the first three the same way: each stretch is as wide as the square root of its time (the rule the page's own envelope drawings already use since 17 Nov), so 8 ms gets a visible rise next to 120 ms; the floor is −48 dB at the foot with no band under it, which takes the wall off the release (the curve meets the foot at −48 dB a hair before its end); and the held gap is a narrow quiet break, a fourteenth of the width, with no rule. They differ in what is drawn with the curve:

i · Line and letters
Just the line in ink, no fill and no grid, with the stages' letters under their stretches (D A H D S R, the S under the held break) and the four readings under it. Changed because the fill and the grid were what made it a chart; the letters do the grid's one useful job, saying which part is which, and they stay legible at 960 × 600 where gridline labels did not.
ii · Filled, grid, dots
Serum's look: a soft fill, a faint grid (−12, −24, −36 dB and the stretches' edges), and the points as small dots, the ones you added in the accent. Changed so the fill is the shape's own wash under a darker line, the grid marks the stretches rather than milliseconds, and the dots say it is made of points you can take hold of.
iii · Values on it
The line with its numbers written where they act: the attack's time beside its rise, the decay's along it, the sustain's level over the held break, the release's along its fall. No grid and no table under it, so the drawing is the reading. Changed because the old block's readings sat in a row under it and had to be matched to the shape by eye. Its cost: at 960 × 600 a short stretch's number can sit beside rather than over it.

The four alternatives

A · In place
No overlay at all. The amp envelope's block is its editor, filling what its column leaves under the Generator; the filter's block is its response and its envelope, both editable. Every gesture of the first mockup works on them (drag, double-click to add or remove, the diamonds, right-click, the keyboard), on the square-root scale. The six knobs are behind Knobs, which swaps them for the readings. Its cost: the drawing is as large as Play's spare room and no larger (see the table), shift-dragging fine detail into a 30 ms attack happens on a small canvas, and with the knobs shown the drawing gives them its height. Sync, zoom and the loop's marker are there but cramped; Delay and Hold are knobs only.
B · Popover
A panel the height of the columns and the width of the neighbouring column, beside the block and pointing at it: over Expression for the amp envelope, over the Generator column for the filter. The block it came from, the other column, the keyboard band and the screen stay in view. Its cost: a column's width is narrow for time (about 270 px of drawing at 1400 × 900), so its knobs wrap to two rows; and it covers the column beside it, which for the filter is the amp envelope.
C · Expand
The section grows in place to fill its column, and its column widens to half the tab while the others narrow; the Generator folds to its heading while the amp envelope is expanded. Nothing floats. Its cost: the whole of Play moves when it opens (the other columns narrow, their menus shorten), which is the thing the bench's real columns were chosen to avoid mid-drag (CLAUDE.md, the layout decisions) - here it happens on a press, not mid-drag, but it still moves controls the eye had found.
D · Strip (my own)
A short, wide editor along the bottom of Play, under the columns, which give up the empty feet they all have (the first mockup measured 65 to 243 px of nothing under them). Why I think it is better than the three: time wants width and level wants little height (a 6 dB grid reads in 150 px), and the strip is the widest place on the Bench and the shortest - wider than B's panel and C's column, as short as A; it covers nothing, so every control on Play, the keyboard band to play with and the screen stay in view and usable while you shape; the block that opened it stays where it was and says editing below; and it is one place for every shaping feature (the filter's curve and its envelope side by side in it; the Envelope card and later the LFOs could open into the same strip), which is one thing to learn, as the EQ's editor is. Its cost: while it is open the blocks shrink to a line of readings, and at 960 × 600 the strip is 168 px, which is enough for the curve and two rows of knobs but not for the first mockup's time labels and tabs.

The filter in each

  • A: the block holds the response (its point draggable, the sweep band shaded) over its envelope, both small; the readings say the cutoff, Q and the sweep; Knobs shows the ten.
  • B: the panel opens over the Generator column: the response on top with its four knobs, its envelope under with its six.
  • C: the filter's column widens to half the tab: the response and its knobs, then its envelope and its knobs.
  • D: the strip holds both side by side: the response and its four knobs on the left, its envelope and its six on the right. The widest of the four for the response.

In every one the amount's band stays: shaded between the resting cutoff and where the envelope's peak takes it, the curve moving while a note is held, and the envelope greyed with does nothing at an amount of nought.

What carries over

From the first mockup and web/docs/mseg-design.md, unchanged: the curve law and its check against the core, the decibel scale for the amp envelope and the Sustain knob (stored linear, so nothing saved changes), the gestures, the six knobs as the values, the storage and the decode pass. Only where the editor opens and how the small drawing looks are new here. Two hands are left out of this page to keep it to the question; each alternative takes the link pill on its heading as the first mockup does.
