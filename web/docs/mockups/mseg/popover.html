The small block

Just the shape: one line with a faint fill under it, the time on the square-root scale so a short attack has a slope, and the level in straight amplitude, so a sustain of −3 dB sits at seven tenths and the shape reads as the ADSR anyone knows. No grid, no letters, no values on it, no dots, no band for the held part (it is a short plateau), no axis words. A gold dot rides it while a note sounds.

a · Readings under it
The four readings in a row under the drawing, as round 2 had them. Nothing is hidden, at the cost of a line of height.
b · Readings on hover
The drawing alone, a little taller; pointing at it or focusing it with the keyboard lays its readings across its foot. Quieter, but a glance no longer tells you the numbers, and a touch screen has no hover (a press opens the panel, which has them).
The filter's block
Words only takes the little envelope box away and keeps Its envelope · sweeps +1.5 oct (or amount 0, does nothing); the envelope is drawn only in the panel. With its envelope keeps the box, in the same plain style. The response keeps its shaded band, without the dashed edge or the point.

The panel

Every one of round 3's guesses about what read as blocky was tried, and these held up when looked at side by side with alt-b1:

  • Square-root time (each stretch as wide as the root of its time, straight inside it, as the small drawings and the page's own envelope drawings do): the 8 ms attack is a slope rather than a wall. The cost is that equal distances are not equal times, so there is no millisecond ruler; the readings and the readout beside the pointer say the times.
  • No frame and no HELD band. The sustain runs on, dotted, to a small tick on the foot marked key up. That tick and the dotted run were the biggest single change in how boxy it looked.
  • Three level lines (0, −6 and −∞ in straight amplitude, read as dB; 0, −24 and −48 in decibels; 100, 50 and 0 per cent for the filter's envelope), labelled quietly at the left.
  • Round handles for the points and the bends, and a soft fill.
  • Delay and Hold are gold rings on the curve (Delay where the silence ends, Hold where the peak ends, just right of the peak while it is nought), shown while the pointer is over the drawing, while one is held or focused, or when its time is not nought. No tab labels, so nothing collides at the top left.
  • One row of small knobs (40 px cells; the filter's four 48 px, so Resonance fits) at the bottom of the panel, always: the owner's choice over hiding them behind a toggle. The value of what is dragged also shows beside the pointer.
1 · Wide and short
For the amp envelope, the panel sits above its block, over Expression and the Generator, two columns wide and as tall as the space over the block, with its arrow pointing down. That is the only place a wider panel fits without covering its own block. For the filter, whose block is at the top of its column, the panel is to its left over the two columns, as tall as it needs, with the response and its envelope side by side. Its cost: the amp envelope's panel is short (see the table), and it covers the Generator, which is the next thing you might want while shaping a note.
2 · Beside (chosen)
Where round 2 put it, over the neighbouring column at the columns' full height, pointing at its block: the drawing over one row of small knobs, always shown. The owner chose it on 18 Nov, with the knobs never hidden. Its cost: it is narrow, so a long release and a short attack share about 250 px.
Loudness drawn
I would pick straight amplitude for the panel, with the Sustain knob, the readings and the readout beside the pointer staying in decibels. Measured on the default shape at 1400 × 900: in decibels the −3.1 dB sustain sits 6 per cent of the way down a 48 dB axis, so the decay is a 6 px fall and the shape is a box; the release runs flat and then drops its last 20 dB in its last eighth of time, the cliff. In straight amplitude the sustain sits 30 per cent of the way down, the decay is a visible curve and the release a smooth fall, the shape Serum 2 and every hardware manual draw. What it costs: quiet levels are crushed to the foot (−30 dB is 3 per cent of the height), so a shape that lives below −20 dB is hard to draw finely, which is where the decibel view is still better, and why it stays a choice rather than going.

What I changed my mind on

  • Decibels in the drawing. Rounds 1 and 2 drew the amp envelope's level in decibels on the reasoning that the sustain knob should be a loudness scale. The knob should; the drawing should not, because a drawing in decibels of an envelope that ends at silence always ends in a cliff. The knob keeps its decibel law and the drawing goes straight by default.
  • A visible held band. I drew the time the key is held as a shaded band so it could not be mistaken for a stretch of time; it read as a box inside the box. A dotted run and a tick say the same thing.
  • Delay and Hold as tabs. Labelled tabs under and over the axis were findable but collided at the start of every shape; rings on the curve that show on hover are as findable once you point at the drawing, and silent otherwise.
  • Millisecond gridlines in the panel. With square-root time there is no honest uniform ruler, and the panel reads better without one; the times are in the readings and beside the pointer.
