Envelopes, drawn

Every envelope a shape you draw, as many points as it needs; on Play a small clear drawing of each, and a large one to work on, opened over the tabs with the screen still in view.

Everything here is live. The curves are the core's: the old envelope's law (a one-pole aimed a fifth past each stage's end, core/include/scope/envelope.h) and the voice filter's (the state-variable filter of wave.h, cutoff and resonance as scope.html maps the knobs), checked against the core compiled, below. What is invented is said where it is. Nothing in scope.html or the core has changed. The design note is web/docs/mseg-design.md.

What to try

  • Press a drawing on Play to open its editor over the tabs; Close or Esc comes back. The Envelope card in the modulation row opens the third.
  • Drag a point. The peak moves the attack's time; the sustain point the decay's time and the sustain level; the end the release's time; a point you added moves anywhere between its neighbours. Delay and Hold are the two tabs under the time axis: drag them right to open the stretch.
  • Double-click the line to add a point there, double-click a point you added to take it away (double-click the peak, the sustain point or the end to put its knob back where it starts). Adding a point changes nothing you hear until you move it: see The curve law, below.
  • Drag the small diamond in the middle of a stretch up or down to bend it; double-click it to straighten it.
  • Right-click a point or a diamond (or a long press, or Shift+F10) for Type a value, Loop from here, Remove, Reset and What is this. Keyboard: Tab to a point; arrows move it (a hundredth of the view sideways, half a decibel up and down), Shift finer, Delete removes one you added.
  • Loop while held loops between its marker and the sustain point for as long as the key is down; drag the marker to another point. Sync turns the time axis into beats at 120 bpm with a grid, and points snap to it.
  • Zoom with the wheel over the drawing, or −, Fit and +; drag the empty paper to pan while zoomed.
  • Turn a knob under the drawing: its stretch grows or shrinks with every point in it, and the Sustain knob moves the sustain point. The knobs are the values, as on the EQ.
  • The voice filter's editor: drag its one point sideways for the cutoff and up and down for the resonance; the shaded band is how far its envelope sweeps the cutoff, and it moves while a note is held.
  • Two hands: unlink a section (its pill, or the editor's) and the other hand's shape is drawn dashed behind, in its ink.

The choices made

On Play
The Amp envelope and the Voice filter become preview blocks: each a drawing that is a button, with its readings under it, and pressing it opens the editor. The amp envelope's drawing is on the same loudness scale as its editor. The filter's is its response from 20 Hz to 20 kHz with its one point, and a shaded band from where the cutoff rests to where its envelope takes it; under it, its envelope small, which goes grey and says does nothing while its amount is nought (today the drawing looks live at nought). The Type menu stays on the block, because it says whether there is a filter at all. The eight filter-envelope knobs and the amp's four leave the tab, so Play has room to spare at both windows (the line under the window measures it).
The level axis
For the amp envelope, in decibels: 0 dB at the top (full scale, the peak), a line every 6 dB, labelled every 12, down to a floor of −60 dB; under the floor a narrow band marked −∞, which is silence, where the start and the end sit. A curve that passes under −60 dB runs along the floor and drops into the band at its end; a point dragged into the band is silence. The Sustain knob turns in decibels too (−60 to 0, its bottom silence), so the top quarter of its travel is the top 15 dB rather than the top quarter of the amplitude. Stored as now: the sustain is still the linear level the engine multiplies by, so every saved sound loads to the bit; only the knob's law and the drawing change. The filter's and the Envelope card's level axes stay linear, from nought to the whole amount, because what they move is already on a musical scale (octaves of cutoff, a modulation depth) and a decibel axis would cramp their small values.
The time axis
Milliseconds, straight, from the note's start to the sustain point; then a gap marked held, of no time, because it lasts as long as the key; then the release from the key's letting go. It opens fitted, and zooms. With Sync, beats at the tempo, with a grid (1/4 to 1/32) the points snap to, and the knobs read note values.
Bends
Each stretch between two points has a diamond at its middle in time, sitting on the curve, and dragging it moves the level the curve has reached half-way, which is what is stored: the fade's drawing's rule, what is dragged and what is stored are one number. The curve through that midpoint is the core's own law rather than the fade's: see The curve law.
Sustain and loop
The sustain point is marked by a dashed line and held: the level stops there while the key is down, and what follows the gap is the release. Loop while held adds a marker on an earlier point; while the key is down the shape plays from the marker to the sustain point and round again, and the release starts from wherever the loop has got to. A loop never jumps: going round, the first stretch after the marker travels from the level the loop is at, with its own time and aim.
Delay and hold
Part of the shape: the delay a stretch of silence before the attack, the hold a stretch at the peak before the decay. Each is a tab under the time axis that drags sideways, and a knob; at nought they take no room in the drawing.
The six knobs
Delay, Attack, Hold, Decay, Sustain, Release, under the drawing, as the values: a setup, undo, the morph and modulation read and write them exactly as the four do now. Once you have added points: each time knob is the length of its stretch, and turning it stretches or squeezes every point inside in proportion, each keeping its share of the stretch's time and its own level. Sustain moves the sustain point's level and nothing else: points you added keep their levels. The points are not knobs: no host, routing or morph reaches them, only a setup, a preset and undo.
Shapes
ADSR (the four as today, the old curves), AHDSR, Pluck, Swell, Gate and Pulse (a loop), each setting the knobs and the points. Presets of the shape only, not of the sound.
The hands
As the EQ: with two hands the link is in the editor's head after the name and on each block's heading; unlinked, the other hand's shape is drawn dashed in its ink behind, and the block on Play draws both.
The filter
Its editor is the filter's response with one point (sideways the cutoff, up and down the resonance), the type as a switch, Key track and Amount as knobs, and beside it its envelope, the same editor as the amp's on a linear axis. The amount is drawn as the band the cutoff sweeps, signed (a negative amount shades below the cutoff), with its octaves written on it; at nought the band is gone and the envelope beside it greys and says so. Key track draws where the cutoff lands for the strip's lowest and highest keys. The resonance's point sits on the curve, which for low and high pass is the resonance's own height (the peak at the cutoff is Q, in decibels); for band pass and notch the curve's height there does not move, so the point rides at 0 dB or the floor and its drag reads Q.
The Envelope card
The same editor, on the linear axis, opened from its card. While it follows the amp envelope (the owner's rule since 17 Nov) the shape is the amp's, drawn dashed and grey with Following the amp envelope in its head; the first edit of a point, a bend or a knob copies the amp's shape into its own and draws it solid in its sage, one step of undo; Follow goes back. Its routings are listed beside it, as the modulation editor has Where it goes.

The curve law

Every stretch is the core's one-pole, as the envelope is today: it travels from its start towards an aim a little past its end, and arrives at its end exactly on time. The old envelope aims a fifth of the attack's and the decay's fall past their ends, and the release 0.2 of full scale under silence; a drawn bend is stored as the level at half the time, and turned into an aim by k = 2 ln(m / (1 − m)) (the midpoint of (1 − e−kt) / (1 − e−k) is 1 / (1 + e−k/2)), which puts the old curves at a midpoint of 0.710. Chosen over the fade's power law (tp, fadeTravel) for three reasons: the old four numbers then become a shape exactly, sample for sample, so no saved sound changes; it costs one multiply-add a sample, the recursion the engine runs now; and it leaves every corner with a finite slope, where a power law starts a slow curve flat and a quick one vertical, which an amplifier hears as a click. It also means that a point added on the line changes nothing: a one-pole forgets where it started, so the two halves of a stretch split on its curve, aiming where the whole did, are the whole. Its cost: the fade's drawing and these differ at the same midpoint (by at most 0.06 of the fall at a midpoint of 0.9), which is a second law in one app; whether the fade should move to this one is an open question in the note.

Entered from anywhere else, as a release is when the key comes up during the attack, or the first stretch after a loop's marker, a stretch keeps its time and its aim and travels from where the level is. That is the old envelope's own rule, generalised, and it is why a loop never jumps.

How it was checked

The curves against the core. The page's levelAt was compared with the core's Envelope class, compiled from core/include/scope/envelope.h and stepped at 48 kHz (checks/env_core.cpp, checks/curves_against_core.py), for the four settings in the table, each held and let go, one in the attack and one in the decay. Two of them agree to 10−12. The other two differ by at most 6 × 10−5 of full scale (−84 dB), and the check says why: the old envelope ends a stage on the first sample at or past its end, which after rounding is sometimes one sample after the drawn time, so the decay starts a sample late. That is the core's behaviour, not the drawing's, and the engine in the design note keeps it, sample for sample. The nulls must miss by ten times the tolerance: the page with the old release's aim made relative (a fifth of its own fall, as the decay's is), and every stretch straight. The filter's drawn response was compared with the core's svfStep run on sines at 24 frequencies for four settings, measuring the steady output over a whole second; the null reads the resonance as the damping.

Open questions

  • The times' ranges. Today the attack stops at 200 ms, the decay at 1 s and the release at 2 s. A drawn shape wants longer (Serum's go to 32 s); this mockup gives the attack, decay and release 20 s, the delay and hold 5 s, on the drawing's square-root scale, so the first 200 ms still has a fair share of the knob.
  • Whether the peak is fixed at full scale (as drawn here, so velocity and Level stay the loudness) or a point like any other.
  • Whether the sustain point can be moved to another point, or turned off so the shape plays through regardless of the key (a one-shot).
  • What the morph does between two setups whose shapes differ: snap at half-way, or move the points when they are as many.
  • Whether the fade's drawing takes this law too, so the app has one bend.
