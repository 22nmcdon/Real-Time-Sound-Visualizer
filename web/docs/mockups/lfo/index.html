What changes

  • The drawing. One cycle, the editor’s width, the level straight from −1 to +1 with three lines (+1, 0, −1) and the fill to the 0 line. Bipolar because the core’s LFO is: lfoStep gives −1 to +1, and a routing moves its knob both ways round where you set it. So 0 is the knob where you left it, and the 0 line says that. A drawing from 0 to 1 would claim the LFO only pushes upwards, which it never has. Changing that would change every patch’s sound.
  • The gestures are the envelopes’. Drag a point. Press the line twice to add a point, and a point twice to take it away. Drag the hollow round on a line to bend it. Right-click, a long press or Shift+F10 opens a point’s menu: its line (Straight · Curve · S-curve · Hold), A new level each pass, and Remove. The arrows move a focused point and Delete takes it away. Points snap to the Grid (4, 8, 16 or 32 to the cycle); Shift drags freely.
  • Two points at one place are a jump, which is how the square and the saws are drawn. The shape repeats, so the last point’s line runs on to the first point of the next pass.
  • The gold dot is where the LFO is now, with a faint gold upright through it, as the envelopes’ note rides.
  • Seven shapes to start from, as small drawings in the tools: Sine, Triangle, Saw up, Saw down, Square, Random steps, Smooth random. The first five are today’s five, played as today. Choosing one sets l0s as now, and the core plays it through waveAt, to the bit. The drawing shows its drawn twin, held to the core: the sine to 1e-15, the saws, square and Random to the bit, and the triangle to 2e-11 (the core’s asin(sin) is that far off a straight line at its peaks). The first edit adopts the twin, so nothing jumps.
  • Under the drawing: Rate (a knob on a log law, 0.01 to 8 Hz, or through the note values while the rate is in tempo), Phase, Rate in Hz · Tempo, and Starts again Free · Each note · Each phrase. Today the LFO has Shape, Sync and Rate and nothing else, so Phase and the start are the only new controls.
  • Where it goes lists each routing as a row: the control and its tab, its depth as a slider and as a number, and ×. A row is the depth row the page already writes. Add a destination is grouped by tab, with Silence at the top, the same switch as in Routings.
  • The card draws the real shape: two cycles, as today’s cards do, with the gold dot. The checks read its path back against the shape for all seven.

Where it opens, measured

Over the tabs, at the bench’s size, where the Envelope’s editor opens. Two layouts were measured at the six windows.

One column (chosen). The tools, the drawing, its knobs, then Where it goes, as the Envelope’s editor. The drawing is held at 2.4:1 (2.6:1 compact), and is shortened by room, never by window size, so that Where it goes always shows at least three routings and Add. It is 804 × 328 at 1400 × 900 and 691 × 225 at 960 × 600. That is 2.4:1 to 3.4:1, and 3.8 to 4.7 times the area of today’s drawing (428 × 150 on the full face, 365 × 96 at 960 × 600).

Two columns, as today. This gives a tower: 486 × 634 at 1660 × 1050, and 1.5:1 at best. Its own column is too narrow for the tools, which take two lines at 1100 × 860. An LFO is a shape in time, so it wants width. The Envelope’s editor found the same and went to one column (play-figures.md). Having both editors alike is a gain of its own.

Nothing moves

Every box in the editor and both cards stays where it is, to half a pixel, at all six windows. That is 38 boxes: the head, its name and reading, each tool and shape, the drawing, every knob and switch, Where it goes, its rows and Add. They were compared across the seven shapes, Hz and tempo, the three starts, Silence, the clock running, a point selected, a note held, and LFO 1 against LFO 2.

The check found three real shifts on the way:

  • Playfair’s 1 is narrower than its 2, so LFO 2’s name moved its reading. The name is one width now.
  • The chip’s reading widened from 0.20 Hz to the note value and pushed the cards along. It is one width, and shows the hertz or the note value.
  • The first fit was measured before the knobs had readings, so a note took 7 px back from the drawing. The fit is now measured after the readings are written.

Null: a reading sized to its words moves the description when the rate goes to tempo.

What I would change my mind on

  • Random as points. A point marked random lands anywhere from −1 to +1, a new place each pass. One such point, held, is today’s Random to the bit. That costs the editor nothing new, but there is no how far: a random point always uses the full range. A range per point (its drawn level as the reach) is the obvious next step if the owner wants gentler randomness.
  • Today’s square is ±0.9, from the generator’s headroom, and its drawn twin keeps that so nothing changes. A new drawn square could go to ±1. It is the owner’s call whether the preset should.
  • The S-curve is a new line law (half a cosine), added because no curve of the envelopes’ one-pole law makes a sine. Two of the three curve laws are then the envelopes’ and one is the LFOs’ own.
  • Smooth random starts flat: its first pass glides from the drawn level, because nothing is drawn before the first point is reached.
