One note to two: what happens to the first note

The owner's report, 16 November, on Harmonic tone: a bass note in the left, then a higher note in the right, then back to just the bass — the first note dips in and out of the ears; its width and level change instantly when another note is added. Every take here is the instrument itself, the core rendered offline through core/tests/tools/render_cpp.cpp, and every option is a scratch copy of the core's headers: nothing in the instrument has changed. The full write-up is web/docs/one-to-two-notes.md.

The phrase in every take: C2 alone from 0.2 s, E4 added at 1.8 s and let go at 3.4 s, C2 alone again until 5.2 s, then its release. Harmonic tone as the bank has it (it plays in Dyad, the mode's default, since it names none), the picture at 96 with its Swell. Stereo, and best on headphones: the issue is between the ears. 16-bit, 44.1 kHz, six seconds each.

What happens today

The bass's place is decided by what else is held

A note alone is drawn as a ring: its right channel is the same note a quarter of a cycle ahead. To the ear that is the widest a single note can be — both ears equally loud and not moving together (coherence nought). A second note changes that, in a different way in each mode:

  • Dyad (Harmonic tone): the left channel is the lower note and the right the upper, so the bass's right ear stops being the bass at all — it is gone from the right at the sample the key goes down. When the E is let go the right becomes the bass again, but its phase has run on at five times the speed all the while, so the bass comes back at whatever angle the release happened to catch: anywhere from the same in both ears (narrow, centred) to opposite (wide, hollow) — never, except by chance, the ring it started as. That is the “doesn't come back the same”.
  • Poly: the lowest and highest notes lean to their sides, full on their own and half on the other, and only a note alone or an inner voice gets the quarter turn. So the bass goes from both ears at full, a quarter apart, to its right ear 6 dB down and moving in step with its left — narrower and to the left — in 4 ms, and back when the E goes. It does come back as it started.
  • A split: each hand is its own figure, so the bass stays a ring whatever the right hand does.

What does not cause it

Ruled out, with numbers

  • Level normalisation by voice count: there is none; a voice is summed at its own level. With the picture off the bass's left ear reads −11.22 dB alone, with the E and after it, in Dyad, to the hundredth.
  • The envelope: nothing is restarted; the bass's left ear does not move by a hundredth of a dB at either event in Dyad. In Poly the voice is kept by its note and never reborn or re-phased.
  • The limiter: at most 0.06 dB of reduction while the two are held (0.3 dB in the split).
  • The picture swinging the image: its Twist turns the figure, which would swing a narrow image between the ears; measured, the balance between the ears moves by under 1.5 dB however the bass came back.
  • The picture changing the timbre at once: it does change it — the E doubles what is lit (cover 0.17 to 0.34) and the loop's Fold and Echo follow — but no faster than the sound's own breathing: Fold moves 0.02 to 0.03 in the 100 ms after the E, where it moves 0.03 in 100 ms of its own; Echo 0.04 to 0.08 against its own 0.19 to 0.24. That is Breathing doing what it says, and it is the same in every version below (the last table).
  • Scan's table: Harmonic tone has Scan off. On the bank's Poly presets it is on, and there it is a real, slower cause: on Tine piano the held bass gets up to 6 dB louder over the 0.4 s after the E, because Scan reads the two-note figure into every voice (0.0 dB with Scan off). It glides with the screen's persistence and is the picture shaping the sound, so no option touches it.
The options

A and B for Dyad, C and D for Poly

A. When the dyad's interval changes, put the right channel back in step with the left (at the new ratio, from the left's phase) and crossfade the old right into the new over 30 ms, so it neither clicks nor jumps. The bass leaves the right as today, but comes back as the ring it started as, every time; and a dyad reached two ways now draws one figure. B. A, and lean as Poly leans: the bass's own quarter turn stays in the right at half while the E is held, and the E's own copy goes into the left at half. The bass stays in both ears; Dyad's figure is sheared.

A. Dyad: the right put back in step, over 30 ms

As today while the E is held. Listen for the bass coming back wide, as it started, however the E is let go.

B. Dyad: A, and the bass kept half in the right with its quarter turn

The bass stays in both ears while the E is held, a little to the left, and comes back as it started.

C. Keep the quarter turn on the outer notes too, not only on a note alone or an inner voice: the bass still leans 6 dB, but keeps its width (coherence nought throughout), and the pair draws an open figure where today it draws a diagonal smear. D. No lean at all: every note on both sides with its own quarter turn, so a note's image never depends on what else is held. The two notes no longer draw against each other: the figure is their two rings summed.

C. Poly: the quarter turn kept on the outer notes

The bass keeps its width while the E is held and only leans left, where today it narrows as well.

D. Poly: no lean, every note keeps its own image

The bass does not move at all; the E sits round it.

Recommendation

A in any case; then B with C, unless the ear wants D

A is a fix: the Dyad's bass should come back as it left, and today it comes back at a chance angle.

B and C make the two modes treat the first note alike: it stays in both ears, keeps its width, and leans 6 dB towards its side while another is held, in 30 ms (Dyad) or 4 ms (Poly). What it costs: Dyad's figure is no longer a pure X against Y; and in both, the bass still leans. D is the one with no change at all to the first note, and it costs the two-note figure. Which of a 6 dB lean and no lean sounds right is the ear's to say.
