Starter presets

24 Nov. Six candidates for the first release, to start from and tweak. None of them is in the bank, and nothing in the page, the core or the bank was changed; nothing is added until you choose. None draws Paint, so each works on the stable site as well as on testing.

You hear the bank converging on one gritty sound, so these were made to differ from it and from each other: one clean and warm, one plucked, one evolving pad, one bass, one for two hands and one that shows the filter's new sweep with Velocity. Each uses the EQ and one or two modules of the rack where the sound wants them, never all at once. In every one, the screen moves the sound: Scan reads the figure back (each key strikes it), and in the two Breathing ones the picture keeps pushing the sound while a chord is held.

How they were measured. Each heading was measured as honestytest.py measures the bank: the page loads the candidate as you would load a preset, hands over the setup code it sends the instrument, and the core plays C4 held ten seconds from five moments after the load, judged by its worst moment. As shipped, 4 Settled and 2 Breathing. Every field each candidate names came back out of the setup the page made, as presettest.py requires of the bank.

How they were heard and seen. The audio is the core (render_cpp) playing the performance written for each from its setup code alone, at the instrument's own level: nothing normalised, so loudness compares across them (every peak is under -3 dBFS). The pictures are the page itself, the performance played into it in real time as a keyboard's MIDI, at moments through it; the time under each is when it was taken. Y-T is the same setup with the display switched to Y-T, played again. Scan reads what the screen shows, so in Y-T the sound is a little different from what you hear here; it ships in X-Y.

Against the bank. One C4 at velocity 100, held three seconds from the load. Brightness is the heard spectral centroid two and a half seconds in. Grit is the share of the power not on the note's harmonics (aliasing, crushing, the picture's noise), in per cent; top is the share above 2 kHz, at the strike and held, since a stepped or crushed wave is harmonic and grit misses it. By these, the bank is mostly clean on a single held note, so the gritty sound you hear is likely in the strike, in chords, or in what Scan does to a figure once it is played on; these numbers do not settle it.

Things to decide. Which, if any, go in the bank, and with what changes. Each goes under the heading it measures as, with its two lines (all twelve to sixty characters) and its description, then write_presets.py and its thumbnail by write_figures.py. Each is a starting point, so turn them freely: a change it does not contain may take it under the other heading, which is not a fault.

Felt piano Settled clean and warm

Listen for A soft, round piano tone, brighter the harder you play
Watch for A rounded ring that brightens as a key lands

A triangle through a gentle low pass that opens a little on each key, more the harder it is played, with a touch of chorus, a warm low shelf and the top rolled off: a felted piano with no grit in it. A little of its wave is read off its own figure, so each key brightens the tone for a moment as it strikes the screen, and a held chord comes to rest.

Try turning: Scan and Strike in the Picture strip are how much the figure answers each key; at Scan 22 it is a touch; turn them up for a strike you can hear on every key. The Filter figure's Velocity and Amount set how much a hard key opens the tone. The EQ's high band (Space & Memory) is the felt itself: lift it towards nought for a brighter, harder piano.

Played: C, A minor, F and G, a second and a half each; four single notes rising from soft (50) to hard (110); then C held over two octaves for four and a half seconds.

Plucked wire Settled plucked

Listen for A bright pluck that rings off into its echoes
Watch for Each pluck flashes on the figure, then fades

A narrow pulse plucked through a resonant low pass whose own envelope snaps shut in a fifth of a second, so every note is a pluck rather than a tone, with a dotted-eighth echo thrown from side to side. Every key strikes the figure where Scan reads it, so the pluck flares as it lands and settles as it dies.

Try turning: The Filter figure's Decay is the length of the pluck (160 ms: shorter is a click, longer a stab); Resonance its twang. The Delay's Mix and Feedback are the room it rings into, synced to the tempo in Perform. Strike in the Picture strip is how hard each pluck flashes on the figure and in the tone.

Played: An arpeggio in A minor and G, a sixteenth note at a time at 100 bpm with an accent on every fourth, into its dotted-eighth echoes; then A3 held three seconds.

Tidal glass Breathing evolving pad

Listen for A glassy pad that swells and turns while held
Watch for The figure folding slowly in and out of itself

A wavetable pad that swells in over most of a second, through a slow phaser and a little reverb, bright at the top. Most of its wave is read off its own figure, and the picture, with its Swell on, pushes the figure as it repeats, so a held chord keeps turning over, slowly, until you let go.

Try turning: The Picture amount is how far the figure pushes the sound while held: lower it and the pad calms, at nought it holds still. The Wave figure's Position picks the glass from the wavetable. The Phaser's Rate and the Reverb's Mix set how wide the tide is.

Played: A minor with a seventh held seven and a half seconds, F major with a seventh seven, and then let go, so its tail and its stillness after are heard.

Round bass Settled bass

Listen for A round mono bass with a soft bite on each note
Watch for A low figure that kicks as each note lands

One voice at a time, gliding between notes: a square with a sine an octave under it, through a low pass that bites on each note and closes to a round tone, the low end lifted and the boxy middle taken out. A little of its wave is read off its own figure, so each note kicks as it lands and then holds still.

Try turning: Glide in the Generator is the slide between overlapping notes. The Filter figure's Amount and Decay are the bite; Resonance gives it a rubbery edge. The Oscillator's Sub (Ink) is the weight under it. The EQ's mid cut at 350 Hz keeps it out of a mix's way; pull it back to nought for a boxier bass.

Played: A bass line in A, an octave jump and a few passing notes, played with some notes overlapping so the glide shows; then a held A2.

Mirrored hands Breathing two hands, layered

Listen for Two voices on every key, each reshaping the other
Watch for Two figures, each the other's wave, never still

Two hands layered, so every key plays both: an FM sine and a softer triangle that swells in behind it. Each hand's wave is read off the other hand's figure and moved by the other's picture, so neither can sound without the other answering, and a held chord goes on changing between them until you let go.

Try turning: Scan reads the other hand's figure here, so turning it up makes the two hands argue harder. Edit one hand at a time from the header's A and B: B, the triangle, swells in behind A. The Picture amount is how much each hand's picture moves the other's sound; at nought the two settle.

Played: A minor held six seconds, then a fifth held under a short melody, then let go.

Velocity brass Settled the filter's sweep with Velocity

Listen for Soft notes mellow, hard notes blare open
Watch for The figure flares wider the harder you play

A pair of saws through a low pass whose own envelope sweeps up three and a half octaves and back, and all of that sweep follows the key: played softly it is a mellow horn, played hard it blares. A little of its wave is read off its own figure, so the strike flares with the sweep, and a held chord settles as the filter does.

Try turning: Play it from a keyboard with velocity: soft notes are a mellow horn, hard ones blare. The Filter figure's Velocity knob is how much of the sweep follows the key (at nought every note blares alike); Amount is the size of the sweep and Attack how slowly it opens. The EQ's mid band at 1.4 kHz is its brassiness.

Played: One chord (G, C, E) struck five times at velocities 35, 60, 85, 110 and 127; then F major held soft (45) and the same held hard (120).
