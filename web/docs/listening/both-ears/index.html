Real-Time Sound Visualizer · a listening study

Both ears

The owner, 16 November, after the options on the one-to-two page: “the solutions given still have the sounds jumping in and out of each ear. This is also especially prevalent with the new note which only appears in one ear when played. Why can't the sound just reach both ears? Would that affect the picture or cause the picture to not be true to sound?”

Why it happens: what you hear is the screen. The left speaker is the screen's X and the right its Y, so a note's place between your ears is its place on the screen, and a second note has to go somewhere. Every fix so far softened the jump and kept that rule. There are two ways out, and both are here to hear. Every take is the instrument itself, its core rendered offline (core/tests/tools/render_cpp.cpp), with each way a switch in a scratch copy of the core: nothing in the instrument has changed. The write-up is web/docs/both-ears.md.

Stereo, and best on headphones. 16-bit, 44.1 kHz. Way 1 and Way 2 come out about 1.0 dB louder than today on the first phrase and 1.5 dB on the chord, since every note is now in both ears at full; a build would take that back with the level.

The two ways

Keep the picture and change what is heard, or keep them one and change the picture

Way 1: what is heard, apart from what is drawn

The screen is drawn exactly as today, and Scan and the whole loop read it as today. What comes out of the speakers is a separate mix of the same notes: each note in both ears, with an image of its own that never depends on what else is held. Three widths:

  • (a) nearly mono: each note the same in both ears.
  • (b) gentle width: each note's left an eighth of its own cycle behind its right — half today's quarter turn — so it is a little wide and centred at every pitch (coherence 0.71). A turn of the note's own cycle rather than a fixed Haas delay, since a fixed delay of half a millisecond would leave a low C almost mono and comb-filter a high one.
  • (c) wide: each note its own quarter turn, as a note alone is today, for every note always.

Way 2: heard and drawn stay one; every note on both sides

Every note on both channels with its own quarter turn, in Dyad and in Poly (the one-to-two page's option D, carried to Dyad), so the speakers are still literally the screen and the ears are steady. What goes is the figure of one note drawn against another, X against Y: the screen draws the notes' rings summed instead, and Scan reads that.

A second, quieter cause: the limiter

The limiter is last of all, on what is heard, and pulls the whole sound down as it gets loud — so a held note gets quieter as others join it, in both ears alike. It does not move a note between the ears, but it is heard. With every note in both ears at full the pair is louder in each ear and it pulls harder:

What Way 1 changes about honesty to the sound

  • The speakers are no longer the screen. A real oscilloscope fed the output would draw a different figure from the one on the screen — the rust figures above. Every note you hear is still drawn, and everything drawn is heard; what is given up is that the heard pair is X and Y. That is the rule making-it-one-instrument.md already relaxed in its Question 2 (“kept as coherence rather than identity: nothing is heard that is not drawn”); P4's “a voice is heard where it is drawn” is what goes.
  • Everything the loop does is unchanged. The picture, its reads, Scan's table and every routing's depth are today's, so the picture still shapes the sound exactly as today: the shaping, Scan and the plane's effects act on the heard notes as they act on the drawn ones.
  • One thread from the heard pair into the loop remains: the limiter's reduction is handed to the picture meter, whose “running away” verdict trips past 6 dB of it. Louder in each ear, Way 1 works the limiter harder on a loud held chord (6.1 dB at most on the plain chord against 4.3 today). It did not change a verdict in any run here; a build would either accept that or hand the meter the limiting the drawn pair would have needed.
What each costs

Way 1 keeps the picture; Way 2 keeps the speakers as the screen

  • Way 1 costs the heard pair being X and Y, and processing: what is heard goes through a second plane (the phaser, chorus, delay and reverb, and the oversampled shaping stage, run twice), and a dyad's two notes each need two voice chains instead of one. Poly's per-note image is a ring each note already has. The picture loses nothing.
  • Way 2 costs the figure. A dyad no longer draws its X-against-Y figure, and a chord's outer notes are no longer the figure's axes: the screen draws rings summed. Scan reads that figure, so every Poly preset sounds different and is measured again, and the bank's thumbnails change.
  • The widths cost nothing different from each other: (a) is the steadiest and the least spacious, and drops the Phase knob's effect on what is heard, since Phase is the angle between the ears; (c) is today's lone note for every note, and the widest; (b) is between.
Recommendation

Way 1, at (b) or (c) as the ear says

Way 1 answers the question as it was asked: the sound reaches both ears, and the picture is not touched.

Way 2 keeps the speakers and the screen one, but by giving up harmony as geometry's figure, the first of the features the plan ranks. Between (b) and (c): (c) is what a single note already sounds like, so nothing you know changes until a second note arrives; (b) keeps a chord narrower and safer summed to mono. The one-to-two page's option A is still worth having for the picture's sake: in Dyad the drawn figure still comes back at whatever angle a release catches, and the picture shapes the sound.
