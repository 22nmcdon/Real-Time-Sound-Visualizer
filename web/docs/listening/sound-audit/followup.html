25 Nov. The owner listened to the first study (below, kept as it was) and disagreed in two places: "Mirror totally acts like a bitcrusher, and the radial settings do basically nothing until they get below a certain point and then it feels like a bitcrusher again." And the smooth Scan prototype: no real benefit to the ear, so it is dropped from the recommendations. The ear was right on both counts, and the first study missed both for reasons that can be named.

What changed, and why the first study missed it
  • Everything the first study measured was one note. The Plane works on the figure after the voices are summed, so its nonlinear stages work on the chord. What they make of two notes is partials at their sums and differences, harmonics of neither, the same rough cloud a bitcrusher's folded steps make. On one held note Mirror is a clean octave up (even harmonics, which is where the first study put it, beside Warp). On a chord it is the grittiest control on the instrument. The phrase the owner heard has overlapping notes, so it showed this and the measures did not.
  • The suspected reason, stepping locked to the note, is right for Snap and wrong for Mirror. A new measure, Stair, reads the steps in the held note's waveform cycle by cycle. Crush, Rate and Snap step; Mirror, Radial and Twist never do (-60 dB, the floor, from every starting sound at every setting). What they do is a second new measure, Chord grit: a held A3 with its major third, and the share of the sound in the gaps between both notes' harmonics.
  • Six or seven places, not 25 per control. The radius was swept at six places from Clip only, and on the base sine, whose figure is a circle that a radial stage cannot bend (its distance from the centre never changes). Swept finely, with a chord, Fold is exactly nothing while the circle is larger than the figure and full crunch soon after it is not.
What the numbers show
  • Mirror crushes a chord harder than Crush does. Chord grit with Mirror on Both: -0.4, -1, -4 and -1 dB from the sine, the saw, the Tine piano and the Slow pad (their own -60, -46, -26, -29), so nearly all of the chord's sound is in the gaps. Crush at 3 bits reads -33, -34, -27, -27; Rate at 1 kHz -8, -5, -5, -7. X or Y alone is -6 to -9. Its Stair is -60 throughout: it never steps.
  • Radial Fold does nothing until the circle is smaller than the figure, then everything. On a chord from the base sine, Chord grit is -60, Radial off to the bit, at every radius from 100 down to 81; then -37 at 75, -10 at 51 and -5 by 38, where it stays to 2. Where that edge falls is the sound's own size on the screen, not the knob: about 78 on the sine, 70 on the saw, 31 on the Tine piano and 15 to 35 on the Slow pad (35 for the chord, 15 for the held note's whole picture). So 23 to 86 per cent of the knob is dead, depending on the sound.
  • Radial Clip is the opposite and no better. On a chord at the base's level it is never off: -27 dB of Chord grit at a radius of 100, rising smoothly to -7 at 2. On the two bank sounds, which are drawn smaller, it is under the floor for 94 to 96 per cent of its travel and acts only below a radius of about 5 to 8.
  • Twist is in the same family, and nobody suspected it. It turns each point by an angle that grows with its distance from the centre, which on one note (a circle) is a constant turn and nothing to hear, and on a chord is as rough as Mirror: -3 dB of Chord grit at either end from the sine and the saw.
  • The stepping family is Crush, Snap and Rate, and Snap and Crush are now one move. With Stair in the space, Crush and Snap are 0.81 to 0.84 alike (0.56 to 0.71 in the first study), and they cluster together at 0.88. Snap's 6 and 5 bits read -36 and -29 dB of Stair on the sine, Crush's 4 and 3 bits -35 and -30. Rate steps too (-38 at 11 kHz to -21 at 1 kHz) but its chord grit is far larger than either's (-8 against -28), so it sits with Scan, not with them.
  • So, by ear on a chord, the bitcrusher family is six controls, not three: Crush, Snap and Rate, which step; Mirror, Radial (Fold and Clip) and Twist, which do not step but crush any two notes together because they act after the voices are summed. From the sine and the saw, Mirror, Radial, Twist and Rate reach -1 to -9 dB of Chord grit somewhere on their travel, Snap and Crush -28 to -29: all of them 17 to 59 dB over the clean chord. The Shaping's Fold adds chord grit only from sounds already rich (-11 to -23 from the saw and the bank sounds, by aliasing), Drive hardly at all: both are inside each voice.
  • Dead stretches (travel under the floor, from the rest outwards, over 30 per cent):
    • Turn: no measurable change to the sound from any starting sound, at any setting. From the sine and the saw it is the same sound to four places; the screen turns, the sound does not.
    • Scale X, Scale Y and Shear: only stereo width, and only from the sine and the saw; under the floor from the bank sounds over all their travel. They are one width control three times.
    • Radius: 23 to 86 per cent dead with Fold, 94 to 96 per cent with Clip from the bank sounds.
    • Warp and Warp at: they act from the sine (from 17 to 22 on Warp's travel) and never reach the floor from the saw, the Tine piano or the Slow pad.
    • Drive: the first 21 to 36 per cent of its travel does nothing, and from the Tine piano none of it does.
    • Crush: nothing from the Slow pad at any setting; from the Tine piano it steps only at 3 bits and under. Its 8 bits is under the floor from all but the sine, and 6 bits from both bank sounds.
    • Fold (the Shaping's) is the good curve to copy: it acts from 7 to 14 per cent of its travel from every sound.
Judgement: revised recommendations

The first study's verdict was "a bitcrusher family of three, Scan not in it". The second half stands; the first does not. The family is six by ear, and the reason is not the loop or the grid but where the Plane sits: after the voices are summed. Every nonlinear thing there crushes chords. That is one decision, not six.

The decision first. Either the Plane's nonlinear stages (Mirror, Radial, Twist, Snap) move inside each voice, where the Shaping is, which keeps what each does to a note (Mirror's octave up, Twist's turn) and loses the chord crunch, or they stay after the sum, and the face says so: "acts on the chord: rough when two notes sound". Moving them changes what the picture draws as well as what is heard, and would need the plan's loop reconsidered, so I would not do it unasked. But nothing else on this list is worth doing before it is decided.

Merge or cut

  • Snap: cut, or merge into Crush. It is Crush on the chord (0.88 alike with Stair in the space), and its two settings are one (0.99). The first study said this already; the follow-up makes it firmer.
  • Mirror: keep it only if it is wanted as the chord crusher, and then say so. If what is wanted is the clean octave up, it belongs in the voice (a per-voice rectifier is the octave-up pedal without the crunch).
  • Twist and Radial: the same call as Mirror. Twist's sign still makes no difference to the sound.
  • Turn: off the sound controls. It turns the screen and nothing heard.
  • Scale X, Scale Y, Shear and Draw width: one width control, as the first study said.

New curves or ranges

  • Radius, the owner's complaint: make it a fraction of the figure's own size (its running peak distance), so 100 is where Fold starts to bite, whatever the level or the number of notes, and the knob's travel is all useful. Today the edge moves with the level and with how many notes are held, which is why it seems to do nothing and then everything. Clip needs the same, and an off at the top: today it is never quite off on a chord.
  • Drive: a curve that starts acting sooner (a gain ahead of the knee in proportion to the level), since its first fifth to third is dead.
  • Crush: 8 bits is close to nothing, and 6 bits from the bank sounds; a menu of 5 to 1 bits, or a knob down to 1, would spend the control's travel where it is heard.
  • Warp and Warp at: worth an ear test on a saw and a bank sound before anything else. They measure as nothing there; if the ear agrees, Warp is a sine-only control and should say so.
  • Rate: 11 kHz and 8 kHz measure alike (distance 5.5 and 5.4 from the sine); one could go.

Dropped: the smooth Scan. The owner heard no benefit, and it never measured as a bitcrusher.

Limits: four starting sounds, and one chord, a major third; a dense chord or a lower note would cross the edges sooner. The cosine groups still treat a direction and not a size, so the ranking of Chord grit below is the better guide to which controls crush. And by "dead" I mean under these measures and this floor: the Slow pad's and the Tine piano's floors are three times the others', because the loop makes them chaotic, so a quiet effect from them is lost in the loop's own movement.
