In short

The suspicion was that most of the controls head for the same place: brighter, buzzier, stepped, like a bitcrusher, because the loop reads the wave back off a grid of cells. Measured, that is partly true and mostly not. There is a bitcrusher family, but it is three controls, not eight. Scan is not in it. And most of the overlaps that are real are elsewhere: four ways to widen, three to darken, two to put an octave underneath.

What the numbers show
  • The bitcrusher family is Crush, Rate and the Plane's Snap. Their directions are 0.56 (Crush and Rate) to 0.71 (Crush and Snap) alike; Snap's two settings are 0.98 alike, effectively one setting. All three make grit, partials in the gaps between harmonics that do not follow the note: Rate up to +57 dB of it, Crush +17 to +30, Snap +6 to +19 and mostly a flatter, fizzier spectrum (+11 to +70 dB of flatness).
  • Drive is not in it. 0.19 to 0.33 alike with the three. On a sine it adds odd harmonics (+0.9 octave brighter) and no grit at all (-60 dB, the floor, at 100); on a saw it darkens slightly (-0.56 octave) and adds a little grit (+7 dB). It is the clean waveshaper it says it is.
  • Fold is between them. 0.46 to 0.51 alike with each. It brightens far more than anything else that is not a filter or FM (+3.7 octaves on a sine), cleanly from a sine and with grit from a saw (+23 dB), since its new harmonics then pass the Nyquist.
  • Scan is not a bitcrusher. 0.36 to 0.48 alike with the three, and its grit at 100 stays at the floor from the sine, falls from the saw (-10 dB) and rises only 4 dB on the Tine piano. What it makes instead is shimmer (sidebands round each partial, +36 to +50 dB), inharmonic partials (+35 to +46 dB), a noisy floor (+36 to +37 dB) and movement while held (spectral flux 0.17 to 0.23, where a still note reads 0). It is a moving, wandering timbre, not a stepped one.
  • The Picture amount and Swell hardly move a held note. Swell's reach is under the chaos floor from every base; the Amount's is under it from the two bank sounds. From the still sine it adds odd harmonics, which is its glow folding the wave (its route onto Fold). What the loop does is mostly in how a held sound moves and settles, which a held note's averages do not see.
  • Things that do the same move, in different sections (the cosine of their directions, 1 the same):
    • Width with Draw width on, the Plane's Scale X and Scale Y down and Shear: 1.00, all only stereo width.
    • The Sub and FM at 1:2 (with 3:2, 5:2, 7:2 and 5:4): 0.91, an octave under the note.
    • The voice filter's Low pass, the EQ's High shelf down and its high cut: 0.87, darker and cleaner.
    • FM, Sync and the voice filter's High pass: 0.90, brighter. The EQ's High up and low cut sit near them.
    • Warp, the Plane's Mirror and the Drawbars shape: 0.92, even harmonics on a sine.
    • Drive, Ring, and the square, triangle and Sine + 3rd shapes: 0.91, odd harmonics on a sine.
    • The amp envelope's Attack and its Delay: 0.98; Attack and the filter's Env amount turned down, 0.93.
    • The Plane's Twist either way: 1.00, the same move whichever sign.
  • Controls that measured as doing nothing from any of the four starting sounds (under each one's null floor): Phase, Tuning, the filter's Key track and Velocity, Warp at, both oversamplings, the Plane's Turn and Limit, the delay's Tone, Width and Ping-pong, the rack's order, Swell, Responds to, Rise and Fall, and a dozen single directions of others (the full list is under How it was measured). Some of these act only on what these measures cannot see - Ping-pong and Turn move the sound between the speakers in time, Key track and Velocity need other notes - so this is "not seen here", not "does nothing".
  • Directions: every direction asked for is reached by something, but not equally.
    • Darker and cleaner: only by taking away - the voice filter, the EQ - or by adding an octave below (the Sub, FM at 1:2), which lowers the centre without smoothing anything.
    • Inharmonic, a bell: reached, and cleanly: FM at 4:3 or 5:3 turns nearly all of a sine's energy into partials that follow the note but are not its harmonics (+59 dB), with no grit from a sine (from the two bank sounds it brings grit too, +24 to +44 dB, since they are already rich). It needs FM above nought and a ratio from a menu, both in the Oscillator.
    • Noisier, breath and air: only by replacing the tone with a noise shape (Noise, Pink, Brown, S&H). No control mixes noise into a tone; the best that adds any are the delay and chorus smearing.
    • Percussive: only the amp envelope's Sustain and Release, and the voice filter closing; the filter's Env amount gives a bloom (+0.46 octave brighter on the strike) but no decay of level.
  • A smooth Scan (prototype only) moves it a little further from the crushers, not a lot: its similarity to them falls from 0.43 to 0.31 on the sine and from 0.56 to 0.40 on the Tine piano, its reach by a tenth to a fifth. It was never in their cluster to begin with.
Judgement: what I would do with it

The owner's ear is right that a lot of the instrument goes one way, but the way is not "bitcrusher". The controls that sound alike are mostly the ones that add partials on top (Fold, Drive, FM, Sync, Crush, Rate, Snap, Scan, Mirror, Warp): ten ways to add, and only filters to take away.

Candidates to merge or cut

  • Snap's two settings (5 and 6) are one sound: keep one, or make Snap a depth.
  • Snap against Crush: Snap is a crusher of the screen's plane, and it does what Crush does, quieter and fizzier. If one goes, Snap; or move Snap next to Crush and say what it is.
  • Width (Draw width) and the Plane's Scale X, Scale Y and Shear all only widen or narrow. Four knobs for one dimension; the Plane's three exist for the picture's sake, so the candidate is Draw width, or saying plainly that Scale is width.
  • The Sub and FM at 1:2 put the same octave under. The Sub is the clear one; 1:2 could leave the ratio menu, or stay as the buzzier version.
  • Three darkening filters (voice Low pass, EQ High, EQ high cut) is a normal synth layout - per-voice and on the mix - and I would keep it, but the EQ's high cut and High shelf are close enough to be one control.
  • The Twist's sign makes no difference to the sound (1.00). Fine for the picture; if Twist is a sound control, it is one direction.
  • The rack's order, the oversamplings, Warp at, Ping-pong measured as nothing here. Worth an ear test each before keeping them on the face: if the owner cannot hear them either, they are candidates to hide.

Candidates to add, in the loop's terms (the plan's first rule is that a feature acts on the loop; each of these could be a destination the picture moves):

  • A way to darken by smoothing, not filtering: a "soften" on the Shaping (a slew, or a low pass inside the wave's own drawing) so there is an opposite to Drive and Fold that is not a filter.
  • Noise mixed into a tone: a breath or air amount on the generator, which nothing does now; the noise shapes replace the tone outright.
  • Inharmonic on the face: the bell is there, behind FM above nought and a ratio menu. A single "inharmonic" or "bell" amount, or ratios on a knob, would put a direction that exists where it can be found.
  • Percussive beyond the envelope: a level drop the picture can drive (the loop already has a strike); the filter's Env amount brightens the strike but never takes level away.
  • A smooth Scan is worth building only as a Softness that reaches further, since that is what it measured as: less shimmer and movement, not a different family.

Limits of the judgement: four starting sounds, one note, a phrase, and features that average over a held note. A control that acts on how a sound moves over seconds, between the speakers, or across notes is seen poorly or not at all, and two controls the numbers call alike can still be told apart by ear, which is what the examples below are for.
