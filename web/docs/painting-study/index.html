A study, 12 November

Painting the sound

Four phrases played through the instrument's own core, and each painted - as oil paint on canvas, and in three other languages - from what the sound contains: a wash where a note is held, a stroke where a melody moves, spatter where a key is struck hard, grain where the sound is noisy, and harmony as which ink.

Nothing in the instrument was changed to make this. The write-up, with what was found and the questions for the owner, is painting-the-sound.md; the scripts that made every file here are in tools/, and run again to the same pictures.

Second cycle, after the owner's look

Where the sound puts the paint

The first cycle put every painting on rings, so every painting looked alike. Every composition is also a sound

What Scan reads off each

Where the paint lands against Scan's circle decides the tone, so each painting above has two sounds under it: Scan's read of the canvas as it is painted, and of the finished painting held. A first approximation, as before: the ink's density (for physical paint, how far the paint hides the ground) on the 64 by 64 grid, read by the core's own scanGeometry, scanBlur (Softness 3), scanRead and scanTables, played as A3 with the table at a depth of 0.6 against a sine. The first cycle's ring layouts are the baseline.

Centroid: the table's harmonic centroid, in harmonics (a sine is 1). Movement: how far the table changes from one frame to the next, nought to one. High: its energy above the eighth harmonic (harsh where large). Sine: its energy in the first harmonic (dead where nearly all).

Physical paint

A brush you can read

Four links, chosen from the pairings people find natural between sound and sight: loudness is size and thickness (the core's level presses the brush wider, a hard strike loads it heavier); pitch is height (the composition's: each hand's brush drifts to its register's height); brightness is lightness and sharpness (white mixed into the dip, stiff hard-edged bristles); roughness is a jagged, broken texture (the line shakes, the brush drags dry). Each stroke adds its own small seeded variation, so the same performance paints the same picture.

And in the paintings, details at full resolution, one pixel to one, from Cycles:

Fixed: the thin, hard lines that shot out at a stroke's end. As a brush lifted, its bristle groups were mapped onto the narrowing contact and squeezed towards the centre line; now each keeps its place on the brush, fewer touch as it lifts, and the end feathers.

Left for a later cycle

Drying

Built before the owner narrowed this cycle, and not judged here (it can only be judged while playing): wetness falls to 1/e in 1.5 × (1 + 3 × thickness) seconds, so thin paint dries before thick. The gloss in these build-ups is the paint still wet.

How to read them

The mapping

The soundRead fromThe mark
Which note, how loud, still heldThe core's own voices, every 2.7 msWhere it lands; a stroke's width (tapering as the level falls)
A held toneA voice's level above a twelfth of its peak, past its attackA wash (or pool, or circle) that grows and deepens as long as it is held
A melodyStrikes in one hand within 0.45 s of each otherOne stroke through them, gliding to each next note
A sharp attackVelocity and the core's attack timeSpatter, drops or rays: how many by velocity squared and sharpness, how far by velocity
AgitationStrikes in the last half second, and how fast the spectrum changesHow far the brush shakes off its line; how far a strike flings
BrightnessSpectral centroid against the sounding pitchHow fast the ink-and-wash brush dries into dry-brush streaks
Noise and gritSpectral flatnessGranulation in washes; specks of grain; a grit of small squares
HarmonyEach note against the lowest sounding, round the circle of fifthsIts hand's own ink (unison, fifth, fourth), a related ink (seconds, thirds, sixths), or the clash ink (semitone, major seventh, tritone)
Each handThe split (Crossed hands only; the others are one hand)Its own three inks

Layered lays the piece round the canvas, one turn every eight seconds clockwise from the top, pitch outward from the centre, so a longer piece paints over its own beginning. Scroll lays it left to right in time, pitch up. Accumulate keeps everything; Fade lets every mark fall to a tenth in four seconds.

  • Physical paint. Oil on canvas, simulated: a bristle brush whose paint runs out along the stroke, pressed wider and heavier by loudness; paint kept as a height map and lit from the side; strokes laid into wet paint drag and mix into it (Kubelka-Munk), strokes laid on dry paint sit on top and dry-brush across its ridges.
  • Ink and wash. Washes that deepen while a note is held; a broad nib at a fixed angle following the melody, drying as it goes; a few drops on a hard strike.
  • Gestural. Poured pools for held notes, which drip when heavy; flung skeins for the melody, thinner the faster the hand moves, overshooting on hard strikes; spatter in the direction of travel.
  • Geometric. A circle for each held note with a halo; arcs for steps and straight lines for leaps; a band round the turn for a consonant chord, a plane across it for a tense one; a dot and rays for each strike.

Phrase

Crossed hands

The owner's repeatable setup (Picture 47, delay, phaser and reverb on, split at C4) and sequence, compressed from 27 s to 15: D♭2 A♭2 C3 held; a right-hand run in D♭ major pentatonic with C, five notes a second at velocity 55 and then eight at 95; the chord changed to G♭2 D♭3 F3 during it; the run stopping; soft B♭-to-C and G♭-to-A♭4 grace notes.

Measured: median level -18.2 dB, brightness 0.243 octaves over the pitch, agitation 0.235, grain 0.101; 46 notes.

A fast run

Tine piano, from the bank: C major from C4 to C5 and back three times at nine notes a second, each pass swelling to its top and falling back, a little louder each time, ending on a held C4.

Measured: median level -20.4 dB, brightness 1.177 octaves over the pitch, agitation 0.857, grain 0.0; 43 notes.

Palettes

Three palettes

The house palette is the six lane inks on the cream, exactly as DESIGN.md has them: the left hand's figure inks charcoal and slate, the right hand's olive and sage, terracotta and plum as each hand's clash. Earth and Night are proposals for a product table, not tokens. Measured as DESIGN.md measures the lane inks: OKLab distance times a hundred, worst of all pairs, with colour vision deficiency simulated (Machado et al., full severity). The palette is only the look: the ink density the paintings are made of was compared across all three palettes and differs by 0.0 (the lightness of the coloured images, the null, differs by up to 0.352).

Memory

Fade

Each painting made in time with its sound, older marks fading to a tenth in four seconds, as the screen's Persistence fades the trace. Two phrases per style; press play for sound.

A first approximation

What Scan would hear

Each style's Crossed hands painting, reduced to a 64 by 64 grid of ink density (for physical paint, how far the paint hides the ground) and read by the core's own Scan functions (scanGeometry, scanBlur at the default Softness of 3, scanRead, scanTables), then played as a held A3 with the table at a depth of 0.6 against a sine. This is not the instrument playing a painting: nothing yet puts a painting on its screen, the voice's other stages are left out, and the density grid is the study's assumption about what Scan would read.
